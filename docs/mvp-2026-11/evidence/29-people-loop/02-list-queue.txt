Rules in force (a tag is judged only when it has rules):
<rules>
Context-dependent
  1. A pronoun with no antecedent inside the claim ("He said ...", "its predecessor", "they announced") makes the claim Context-dependent.
  2. A definite reference that needs an outside antecedent to identify it ("the vulnerabilities", "the discussions", "the recording", "the settlement terms", "the legal dispute", "recent actions") makes the claim Context-dependent, even when other entities in the claim are named.
  3. A descriptive definite that names a kind of thing but not which one ("the Bitcoin ETF", "the major-bank stablecoin consortium", "the open-access version", "the infrastructure", "the new model") makes the claim Context-dependent: the reader must already know which ETF, consortium, version or product is meant. Applies to countable things, not to abstract or mass nouns ("the regulatory clearance", "the market").
  4. Test: could a reader who sees only this sentence say which specific thing each definite reference points at? If any answer is no, tag Context-dependent.
  5. Do not use for indefinites, time expressions, generics, or definites the claim itself resolves.
</rules>
