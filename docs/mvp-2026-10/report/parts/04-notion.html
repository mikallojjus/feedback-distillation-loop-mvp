Notion walkthrough

In short

  • Everything lives on one Notion page, "Editorial feedback loop". At the top a callout says what the trial is, where editors work, that nothing is written to Geo, and how to pause the agent. Below it sit the views Your queue, Needs your note, Lessons waiting for the rule owner and Latest runs, and since the editor review one for rows handed on to someone else.
  • An editor works in Your queue: read the claim and the agent's reason, set the Tag outcome, and add a Why wrong value with one sentence only when overruling the agent or the written rule. Each decision that settles a tag is kept as a regression case, a test for every later rule change.
  • The rule owner works in Lessons waiting: approve or reject each lesson, and rewrite its text if needed.
  • Everyone can read Latest runs: one row per agent step, and for every gate a result card that says what was tested, what changed and why it was or was not adopted.
  • At the end of the trial: {{count:stage.end.toReview}} rows wait in Your queue, LS-1 is Approved but held at the gate, the planted LS-2 is Rolled back, and the rulebook is unchanged, still at rubric version {{count:rubricVersion}}.

How to decide a row

  1. Read the claim (the row links to it) and "Why I recommend this". Agent confidence runs from 0 to 1 and is not calibrated: it sorts the queue, it is not a probability.
  2. Set Tag outcome yourself, whatever the agent proposed: Applies, Does not apply, Needs evidence (a source must be read first) or Ask another editor. Agreeing with Add tag or Keep tag means Applies; agreeing with Do not add tag or Remove tag means Does not apply.
  3. Only when you overrule the agent or decide against the written rule, pick one Why wrong value:
    • Agent slip: the rule settles it, the agent did not follow it.
    • Rule unclear: the rule does not settle it.
    • Rule should change: the rule settles it but is wrong.
    • No tag fits: no rulebook tag names the problem.
    • Other: none of these.
  4. Write one sentence in Your reasoning for every Why wrong value except Agent slip. Lessons are drafted from these sentences; Needs your note lists the rows that still lack one.

That is all: Stage and Direction update themselves, the agent never changes these fields, and a decided tag is never overwritten on its claim.

Changed after the editor review. The first reviewer could not tell whether to fill Decided at and Decided by, or what each Stage and Direction value means. The column descriptions and the hub's callout now say it: Tag outcome alone moves the row, the two Decided fields are optional, every Stage and Direction value has a one-line meaning, and the callout states how a proposal maps to an outcome (Verification and reviews).

Proposed textFires on an unconditional future/forecast marker: will, is expected to, is forecasted to, is projected to, is set to ("the market is forecasted to grow to a multi-trillion size by 2030", "the largest wave of maturities is expected in the fall"). Hedged markers (is likely to, is likely, probably) fire only when they attach to a definite future outcome or event that only the future can settle ("the hiring freeze is likely a short-lived phase" fires). Hedged markers do NOT fire when they describe: an actor's general tendency or disposition, which can be checked against its past record ("the agency is likely to side with large incumbents"); a comparison of which strategy or approach tends to work better ("tariffs are more likely than talks to change the policy"); or a present trajectory ("is on track to", "is heading toward"). Such claims are assessments of the present, not forecasts.

What the connector's DDL taught

  • A RELATION takes the bare data-source UUID, not a collection:// link.
  • A unique-id prefix needs at least two characters, so lessons are LS-1, LS-2 rather than L-1.
  • The DDL parser splits formula text at a quote, comma, space, quote sequence: write "," inside a formula without the space after the comma.
  • A formula cannot read a property through a relation (current.prop() is a type error). Tag name and Lesson status are therefore rollups, and the two Claims columns roll up the Applied tag and Rejected tag formulas of the queue rows.
  • Column names avoid apostrophes ("Editor wording", not "Editor's wording"); editor guidance lives in the column descriptions, since there is no start page.
